## 1. Mission and philosophy

The Lumenary is a free, open-source mathematics curriculum, designed to run from kindergarten to the first year of graduate school, with optional research pathways. It is inspired by historic Christian values — truthfulness, humility, generosity, love of neighbour — but requires no belief of any learner, and justifies every mathematical statement mathematically.

Its pedagogical commitments are: understanding over memorisation; productive struggle with progressive hints; no shame-based feedback, compulsory streaks or rankings; respect for different learning speeds; accessibility; and no paid subscriptions. The guiding principle is *Master the present. Glimpse the future. Never require the future to understand the present.*

## 2. Three integrated modes of reasoning

Every lesson interleaves **Human** reasoning (intuition, examples, conventional proofs), **Formal** reasoning (Lean 4 definitions and machine-checked proofs) and **Computational** reasoning (Python experiments with explicit labels for the kind of evidence produced). A "lens" control lets a reader highlight one mode at a time. For young children the formal mode becomes age-appropriate logic games; Lean code appears only in collapsible "for grown-ups" panels.

## 9. Accessibility overview

- Semantic landmarks, a skip link, visible focus styles and keyboard operation of every interactive exploration (no drag-and-drop is required).
- Mathematics is rendered with KaTeX including MathML output for assistive technologies.
- Colour is never the only carrier of meaning: modes, statuses and feedback also use text and icons. Light and dark themes; reduced-motion preferences are respected.
- Feedback regions use live announcements; exercises accept regional numerals (e.g. Eastern Arabic or Devanagari digits).
- Known gaps: no formal accessibility audit has been carried out, and the interactive diagrams have not been tested with screen-reader users.

## 10. Internationalisation overview

- Six demonstration interface languages spanning several language families, scripts and both writing directions: English, Spanish, Arabic (right-to-left), Hindi (Devanagari), Simplified Chinese and Swahili.
- Only English is canonical. All other translations are machine-generated drafts, labelled as such, and **none has been reviewed**. Untranslated content falls back to English with a visible notice and the correct language attribute.
- A machine-readable language registry (BCP 47 tags, scripts, direction, status) and a registry of every UN member state with its official or de facto languages are included, with uncertain entries flagged for review.
- Six demonstration locales are not global coverage. That remains a long-term goal.

## 11. Known limitations

- Only five lessons and seven Wonder Trails exist; every other topic in the map is planned, not written.
- The formal library uses core Lean 4 only; Mathlib integration is planned. Lean exercises are solved locally or in the public Lean web editor, not inside the site.
- Python runs in the browser via Pyodide, which downloads several megabytes on first use and needs a reasonably modern browser.
- Progress tracking is local to one browser by design; there are no accounts or cross-device sync.

## 12. Areas requiring human review

1. **Mathematical accuracy** of every lesson, trail, exercise and solution — especially definitions, theorem statements and proofs.
2. **Pedagogy and age-appropriateness**, particularly of the K–2 and Grades 3–5 lessons.
3. **Historical claims** in the "History and global context" sections.
4. **All translations** and the mathematical terminology glossaries.
5. **The country and language registries**, especially entries marked as needing review.
6. **The curriculum map**: topic placement, levels and prerequisite edges.

The Lumenary is an independent project and implies no institutional endorsement.
